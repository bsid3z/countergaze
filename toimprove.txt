===============================================================================
 SquachWatch — how the BLE side works, what is missing, and what to do next
===============================================================================

 A working note, not a spec. Everything here was read out of the source or
 measured, and the file/line references are there so the next person can
 disagree with the conclusions rather than re-derive the facts.

 Written against this fork (bsid3z/countergaze) of skizzophrenic's
 SquachWatch-CYD. Most of what follows applies to upstream unchanged.


===============================================================================
 PART ONE — HOW IT SEES BLE AT ALL
===============================================================================

 src/detection.cpp:90   class BleScanCallbacks : public NimBLEScanCallbacks

 Two entry points, and which one is used matters:

   onDiscovered()   fires on EVERY advert, before any reply
   onResult()       fires once the library has a device's full data

 Counting happens in onDiscovered, deliberately. The comment in the source
 gives the measured reason:

     "counted at onResult, 200 unanswering adverts a second read as a
      quiet room right up to the crash"

 In an active scan a device that never answers is not handed to onResult
 until the reply times out — so the devices that fill the heap are exactly
 the ones a count at onResult cannot see.

 From each advert it reads:
   - the advertiser's MAC              -> OUI lookup (77 entries)
   - 16-bit service UUIDs              -> UUID table (15 entries)
   - manufacturer data: company ID     -> company table (7 entries)
     and then the payload              -> iBeacon / Find My / Remote ID checks
   - the advertised device name        -> name matching
   - RSSI                              -> signal meter, proximity logic

 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 The scan mode adapts, and it is about heap, not about threats
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 src/detection.cpp:838-860

   SCAN_ACTIVE_BELOW    50 adverts/s   quiet enough to start asking
   SCAN_PASSIVE_ABOVE  300 adverts/s   too loud to keep asking
   SCAN_ACTIVE_BLOCK_B 8192 bytes      the free block an active scan needs
   SCAN_MODE_SETTLE_MS 5000            listen this long before the first change
   SCAN_MODE_DWELL_MS  30000           and this long between changes

 Passive is the default and the safe state. Active costs heap because
 replies get buffered; under a flood that was eating a 14 KB block in under
 a second. The 300 figure was raised from 90 after measuring roughly 3 KB
 of cost under a 200-a-second flood.

 What passive loses: only what a device says when ASKED — some names, and
 a squad message.


===============================================================================
 PART TWO — THE GAP: BLE SPAM IS MEASURED, SURVIVED, AND NOT REPORTED
===============================================================================

 This is the single biggest hole in the product, and it is a small one to
 close because the hard part is already built.

 THE ASYMMETRY
   WiFi attacks are detected behaviourally, with no signature at all:
     DEAUTH    6 deauth/disassoc frames in 3000 ms, 15000 ms cooldown
     EVILTWIN  one SSID from two BSSIDs disagreeing about encryption
   BLE attacks are not detected at all. There is no BLE_SPAM in
   DetectionType (include/state.h) and nothing in detection.cpp looks for
   one. Checked; it is absent.

 WHAT ALREADY EXISTS
   s_advRate (src/detection.cpp:855) is adverts/s over the last second.
   s_advKind[] counts adverts by HCI advert type in onDiscovered.
   The code was explicitly tested against spam — the source says so:
     "measured, the fake flood plus a Flipper"

 SO TODAY
   A Flipper running BLE spam next to you:
     - IS caught as HACKER, if it also advertises its own UUIDs / company ID
     - the SPAM ITSELF is invisible. The board quietly switches to passive
       scanning and says nothing.

   For a device whose whole purpose is announcing hostile radios in the
   room, that is the wrong silence.

 WHY IT IS THE RIGHT NEXT FEATURE
   - the measurement exists; only the decision and the alert are missing
   - DEAUTH is the pattern to copy, including the cooldown
   - behavioural, so it catches tools nobody has a signature for yet
   - one new DetectionType, one threshold, no new tables, no flash to speak of

 WHAT HAS TO BE GOT RIGHT FIRST (do not skip this)
   A detector that cries wolf in a train station is worse than one that
   says nothing. Before writing code, establish:
     - what a busy but innocent room actually reads (a café, a train, a
       conference). The existing console line already prints adverts/s, so
       this is a walk with a board, not a guess.
     - whether to threshold on RATE alone or on the shape of it. Real spam
       is typically many EPHEMERAL advertisers — a flood of MACs each seen
       once — rather than a few loud ones. Distinct-MACs-per-second is
       probably the stronger signal and is cheap to count.
     - the Apple/Samsung/Google pairing-popup variant is a payload shape,
       not just a rate: many adverts carrying those company IDs with
       nonsense payloads. That is a second, higher-confidence detection.
     - a cooldown, so one event is one alert.
     - interaction with the passive switch: above 300 adverts/s the scan
       goes passive, which is exactly when spam is happening. Make sure
       the detection does not depend on data passive mode cannot see.

===============================================================================
 PART FOUR — MESSAGES (SQUACHMESH)
===============================================================================

 Boards talk INSIDE BLE advertisements — the same packets the scanner is
 already reading. No connections: central and peripheral roles are compiled
 out, so the firmware cannot open or accept one.

   include/squachmesh.h:33   COMPANY_ID = 0xFFFF
   include/squachmesh.h:37   MAGIC = { 0x53, 0x51, 0x4D, 0x31 }  "SQM1"  presence
   include/meshmsg.h:118     MAGIC = { 'S', 'T' }                        messages

 Encrypted with a key derived from the five-word phrase and the salt
 "SquachWatch/msg/v1", with replay counters that survive a crash, and a
 cipher self-test against known vectors at every boot.

 WORTH KNOWING: 0xFFFF is officially

   "Bluetooth SIG Specification Reserved Default Vendor ID for Remote
    Devices Without Device ID Service Record"

 — the reserved/unassigned value. A legitimate choice for a non-commercial
 protocol, but it means SquachMesh adverts are indistinguishable BY COMPANY
 ID from anything else using the reserved value. The SQM1 magic is what
 actually identifies them, which is exactly why squachmesh.h says MAGIC is
 not optional.

 PART FIVE — WHAT THE BLUETOOTH SIG REGISTRY SAYS ABOUT OUR SIGNATURES
===============================================================================

 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Use the live YAML, not the PDF
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   OFFICIAL, machine-readable, current:
     https://bitbucket.org/bluetooth-SIG/public/raw/main/assigned_numbers/
       company_identifiers/company_identifiers.yaml     4035 entries
       uuids/member_uuids.yaml                           714 entries
       uuids/service_uuids.yaml                           76 entries

   The Assigned_Numbers.pdf on bluetooth.com is dated 2023-12-20 and is NOT
   good enough for this. Three vendors the firmware already ships are absent
   from it entirely: Meta Platforms Technologies, Luxottica, Flipper Devices.
   Used as a verification source it would have declared three CORRECT
   entries wrong.

   The popular NordicSemiconductor/bluetooth-numbers-database mirror is
   usable but was 14 assignments behind the official YAML when checked.

 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Company IDs: all seven verify, zero mismatches
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   0x004C  Apple, Inc.
   0x09C8  XUNTONG                                  (Flock's BLE radio supplier)
   0x01AB  Meta Platforms, Inc.
   0x058E  Meta Platforms Technologies, LLC
   0x0D53  Luxottica Group S.p.A
   0x03C2  Snapchat Inc
   0x0E29  Flipper Devices Inc.

   And the warning in signatures.cpp is confirmed:
   0x0FBA  Cosonic Intelligent Technologies Co., Ltd.  <- headsets, NOT Flipper.
   ESP32 Marauder uses 0x0FBA and everything that copied it inherited the bug.

 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Service UUIDs: only 5 of 15 are SIG-assigned
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   SIG-assigned and verified:
     0xFD5F  Meta Platforms Technologies, LLC   (Ray-Ban Meta)
     0xFD5A  Samsung Electronics Co., Ltd.      (SmartTag)
     0xFEAA  Google LLC                         (Find My Device / Eddystone)
     0xFEED  Tile, Inc.
     0xFEEC  Tile, Inc.

   NOT SIG space — vendor-chosen values with no registry backing:
     0x3081-0x3083  Flipper
     0x3100-0x3500  Raven
     0xFFFA         OpenDroneID  (convention, not an assignment)
     0x1101         SPP — a Bluetooth CLASSIC SDP service class, a different
                    registry entirely. Correct as used; just not this one.

   The SIG member UUID allocation range is 0xFC26-0xFEFF. Everything above
   is outside it. This is not a bug — it is provenance, and it maps onto the
   confidence grading the project already does per signature. An audit could
   surface it per row instead of leaving it implicit.

 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 Coverage: the absences are the interesting part
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   These have NO BLE company ID registered at all:
     Verkada, Avigilon, Dahua, Genetec, Flock, Eufy, Reolink, Ubiquiti,
     Pebblebee, Hak5, Bosch Security

   That is not a hole in our table. Cameras are WiFi devices and do not
   advertise over BLE, which is precisely why the 77-entry WiFi OUI table
   exists and is the right tool. It also explains 0x09C8: Flock has no
   company ID, so the firmware keys on their BLE radio supplier instead.

   Registered and currently unused (candidates for NAMES, not detections):
     0x0E25  Hangzhou Hikvision Digital Technology
     0x0C19  Arlo Technologies, Inc.
     0x0870  Wyze Labs, Inc
     0x04EC  Motorola Solutions
     0x08C3  CHIPOLO d.o.o.

   CAUTION: 0x023E is "Raven Industries" — agricultural equipment, NOT the
   Raven gunshot detector. A tempting name match that would be simply wrong.

 PART SIX — WHAT TO DO, IN ORDER
===============================================================================

 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 TIER 1 — offer upstream now. Finished, tested, fixes upstream's own code.
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   fillSpan + fillColumn      fillRect did one VIRTUAL drawPixel per pixel.
                              Frame drawing 225 ms -> 86 ms on the S3; every
                              CYD and the emulator gain proportionally.
   fillprim_test              those primitives had ZERO coverage —
                              shim_fidelity_test never calls a fill. The new
                              test is proven to catch an injected off-by-one.
   randomSeed(esp_random())   GPIO34 is not an ADC pin on an ESP32-S3, and a
                              floating pin was a poor seed where it did work.
   Doc fixes                  docs/FAQ.md says MIT; LICENSE is GPL-3.0.
                              Someone could relicense in good faith. Plus 3
                              broken links, ALPR 6->7 OUIs, and
                              "76 rows / 32 High" -> 77 / 33.

   The licence one is worth telling skizzophrenic about regardless of
   whether any of the rest is wanted.

 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 TIER 2 — the work actually worth doing
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   1. BLE SPAM DETECTION. See PART TWO. The measurement exists; the
      detection does not. Biggest real gap in the product. Scope the
      thresholds against real rooms BEFORE writing code.

   2. SIGNATURE AUDIT SCRIPT. tools/, build-time only, zero flash, zero
      runtime. Pulls the official SIG YAML and checks every company ID and
      UUID in signatures.cpp against it, and flags which UUIDs are outside
      SIG space. Turns the Flipper-vs-Cosonic comment from something someone
      has to remember into something CI enforces.
      NOTE: it fetches over the network, so in CI it should WARN, not
      hard-fail — otherwise Bitbucket being briefly unreachable breaks a
      build that has nothing wrong with it.

 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 TIER 3 — worthwhile, not urgent
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Vendor names in RAW SCAN. A curated few hundred company IDs, ~5-8 KB, so
   an unknown advertiser reads "Bose" instead of a number. Must be a SEPARATE
   table consulted only by mfgIdName(); lookupMfgId() must not see it, so it
   is structurally incapable of creating a detection or moving a confidence
   grade. Flash headroom as of this writing: 79.9% of the 1.92 MB slot used.

   Windows test portability. src/signatures.cpp uses strcasestr (glibc) and
   gfx/TFT_eSPI.h wants -D_USE_MATH_DEFINES on MinGW. Two small fixes would
   let Windows contributors run the host suite at all.

 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
 WHAT TO SKIP, AND WHY
 -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
   Adding company IDs as DETECTIONS. A company ID means "this vendor's radio
   is nearby", not "this device is here" — the exact trap META already
   documents, where Meta's company IDs match Quest headsets and are why META
   is not graded High. Doing it wholesale manufactures low-confidence
   detections, which is what the project's per-signature grading exists to
   avoid.

   Renaming anything. The salt, the NVS namespace and the log filename all
   carry the project's name and none of them is a name.
